# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Hindu adults around 25–40 who want a private, thoughtful place to talk through everyday relationships, work, family, and life decisions. They may be religiously inclined and may not have someone they feel comfortable talking to. The interface should feel simple to a person who does not use many apps.

## Product Purpose

Saarthi is a private conversation companion that listens, automatically remembers selective, lasting details the user explicitly shares, and offers candid, Gita-inspired perspectives that help the user find a practical next step. Parth is Saarthi’s named AI companion. The home screen is a quiet temple scene with one clear voice action and a smaller Type action. LiveKit supports live calls with interruptions, captions and a greeting chosen from recent permitted context; English, Hindi and Hinglish are provided through the configured speech pipeline; natural Hindi/Hinglish answer and voice quality still need evaluation. Text, voice notes and spoken replies remain available. Continuing a concern over successive visits is the core product promise. It is not a therapist or a divine authority. A small verified Bhagavad Gita collection supplies original Sanskrit and our Hindi/English renderings; broader scripture coverage is future work. Quotations must come from that collection.

## Brand Personality

Calm, clear, and familiar. Saarthi is an original AI companion, not a deity or a person with real memories. It understands the situation before challenging an assumption, asks one useful question at a time, and leaves decisions with the user.

## Anti-references

- No clinical claims, miracle promises, fear-based religious pressure, or claims that advice is divinely delivered.
- No generic chatbot dashboard, crowded spiritual marketplace, human-guru claims, or ornate decoration that competes with the conversation. The supplied calm temple portrait is the current scene; it is clearly labeled AI and does not establish real priestly credentials.
- No manipulative streaks, guilt prompts, or engagement loops that reward longer conversations.

## Design Principles

1. Make starting a conversation feel as easy as speaking to a trusted friend.
2. Put the conversation first; keep history and memory controls easy to find.
3. Use the Vrindavan and Dwapar-era atmosphere through a simple diya-and-lotus mark, Sanskrit, and restrained devotional accents, without depicting Krishna as the product persona.
4. Be candid and kind: understand first, then offer one clear perspective or question.
5. Make privacy choices visible and reversible; audio is processed for the current request and is not saved by the app.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use readable contrast, generous touch targets, clear labels, keyboard-aware chat entry, screen-reader names for icon buttons, and reduced-motion behavior. Hindi and English should fit without truncation; let the system render Devanagari with its native fallback font.
