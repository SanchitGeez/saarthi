# Saarthi: a conversation that continues

Saarthi opens into a calm temple setting with a familiar young companion. The person taps **Baat karein**, speaks naturally, and can interrupt. On a later visit, Saarthi gently offers to continue a relevant concern. The product promise is **a private place to think clearly and work through something over time**. The scene makes starting feel inviting; thoughtful conversation and follow-through give people a reason to return.

This is a proposed product and implementation plan, researched on October 3, 2026. The canvas and clickable prototype show the proposed controls and states. The supplied image is a mood reference displayed through a crop; it is not a finished character asset. Prototype transitions simulate a call and do not connect to a microphone or model.

## Product judgment

The strongest initial customer hypothesis is a Hindu adult who finds their tradition meaningful and wants to talk privately about work, relationships, family expectations, or a recurring difficulty. Start recruiting with one concrete situation, such as a difficult family conversation, while allowing people to bring other concerns. Age and religious identity alone do not establish demand. Test actual return and payment before expanding the feature set.

The scene should feel like sitting beside someone who gives you space. A normal, educated, rooted young adult suits the founder's direction. Keep **Saarthi** as the name and a small **AI companion** label. The image's title “Pandit Ji” implies a credential and authority that the product has not established. The Krishna-inspired calm can live in the atmosphere and visual character without making divine identity part of the product.

The first visit should produce a useful observation or question quickly. Begin broadly enough to let the person choose: “Namaste. Aaj kis baat par baat karein?” A voice response should usually be one or two short thoughts, then room to speak. The existing text prompt's 100–200 words is unsuitable as the default spoken turn. Detailed answers remain available when requested.

## Simple interaction

Opening the app shows the scene, menu, AI identity, **Baat karein**, and a smaller **Type instead** option. Opening the app alone does not start microphone capture or a paid room. Tapping the start button requests permission if needed and connects. Once the agent and audio are ready, the companion can greet first exactly once. After connection, conversation is hands-free: no tapping for every turn.

During a call, show the companion, one truthful status, readable captions, **Mute**, **End**, and **Type**. Keep topic categories, About, Share, Save, and view selection out of the main scene. Captions can be hidden from the drawer; do not make them hard to access. The existing drawer remains the place for history, memory controls, language, privacy, and account settings. Drawer history entries open the existing transcript view; optional text input in a live call joins that call's context rather than launching a second assistant at the same time.

End returns to the quiet scene. A brief optional recap can surface one chosen next step, with edit or dismiss, but the ordinary user never has to manage a task board. Do not treat every conversation as coaching: sometimes listening is the useful outcome.

Start the first pilot with foreground calls. If the app backgrounds or receives a phone call, stop capture and pause/end cleanly; explain this behavior. Background calling and lock-screen controls are a later native integration, requiring deliberate audio-session work. Do not advertise them before testing.

## Character states

Use bundled still assets for the first release, retaining face, clothing, camera, temple, and lighting across poses. Prepare **idle**, **listening**, **thinking**, and **speaking** assets; use the calm idle asset with a visible notice for connection problems. A questioning expression can be a speaking variant. An attentive still can be more convincing than a mouth animation that fails to match the voice. A still-image scene does not require a video stream or avatar service.

The state reducer combines connection status, mute status, user speech, agent processing, and actual playback. Connection failure overrides all animation. User speech switches to listening and interrupts playback; agent generation switches to thinking; actual audio playout selects speaking; otherwise use attentive idle. Debounce visual switches and honor reduced motion. Asking is dialogue intent, not a separate transport state. Asset selection should never depend only on what the LLM says it is doing.

Random activities are a later experiment confined to the unconnected scene. During a personal disclosure, looking away or performing unrelated actions could feel dismissive. Avoid random chatter to fill silence. If silence persists, one gentle invitation is enough, followed by patience and an explicit idle timeout.

## Continuity without burden

Persistent relationship context belongs in PostgreSQL. A live worker runs for a connected session, then stops. Future check-ins are durable scheduled jobs. No user needs an always-running model or room between visits.

Keep four concepts distinct: **conversation** (what was said), **memory** (selected lasting facts), **ongoing concern** (what the person is working through), and **follow-up** (a check-in they accepted). The current memory policy deliberately excludes temporary emotions and everyday actions. Record an agreed next step in an ongoing concern rather than weakening that policy and saving every detail as permanent memory.

Greeting policy is selected by application code; the model phrases it naturally. Use previous session end time, recent concern freshness, user-stated action, topic sensitivity, private-mode setting, and whether the greeting has already played for this session. These timings are initial hypotheses:

Elapsed time guides tone; it does not reveal what happened. Never assume the user followed advice or that a relationship is unchanged. Avoid speaking sensitive remembered details aloud before the person invites that topic. Private sessions read no shared memory, create no shared concern, and schedule no follow-up; retained transcript behavior stays explicit and distinct from memory-off.

For a relevant concern, quietly track the person's own description, chosen next step, reported outcome, and current status. Offer “Would you like me to ask about this next time?” or a specific time when useful. At the next visit, offer continuation and allow a fresh subject. Notification check-ins are optional and neutral on the lock screen; do not include personal problem details. No guilt about absence and no fabricated off-screen activities such as “I have been thinking about you.”

## Recommended stack

Use **LiveKit Agents Python and LiveKit Cloud media for the first pilot**, subject to a successful Android native compatibility spike. Keep FastAPI for authentication and durable application data, PostgreSQL/pgvector for owned memory, and the existing Expo client. The frameworks supply call mechanics; the product supplies continuity and discretion.

The [LiveKit Expo guide](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/sdk-platforms/expo/) lists `@livekit/react-native`, `@livekit/react-native-webrtc`, `@livekit/react-native-expo-plugin`, `@config-plugins/react-native-webrtc`, and `livekit-client`. Add `expo-dev-client` for native development builds. [Pipecat's React Native guide](https://docs.pipecat.ai/api-reference/client/react-native/overview) supplies its own native transport paths. Neither works in Expo Go. Neither guide verifies this repo's exact Expo 57 / React Native 0.86.3 combination: pin tested versions only after native builds and real audio-routing checks.

The initial pipeline candidate is **streaming speech recognition → streaming Gemini text model → streaming ElevenLabs speech**, with tested turn detection. It retains control of the chosen voice, scripture tools, and text trail. Benchmark ElevenLabs realtime transcription against a suitable Indian-language provider such as Sarvam and a confirmed Hindi-capable Deepgram configuration. Support labels do not establish within-sentence Hinglish quality: test “haan”, English company names, Hindi clauses, pauses, and background noise.

Use `livekit-agents` with Google and ElevenLabs provider plugins and either its current supported audio turn detector or a tested local detector configuration. Keep provider IDs configurable; do not hard-code preview model names into the product architecture. The [current turn detector docs](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector/) list Hindi for the audio detector. Hinglish still needs evaluation. [Pipecat Smart Turn](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn) is an alternative detector/pipeline ecosystem; framework code and model weights have separate licenses to check before distribution.

An October 2026 integration detail: [LiveKit retired ElevenLabs from hosted Inference on August 31, 2026](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/models/stt/elevenlabs/). Its provider plugin still works with a direct ElevenLabs account. Supply the project's key explicitly through settings and account for a separate bill; do not assume the framework's environment variable names match the existing ones. Configure realtime STT explicitly; the documented default can be batch transcription.

Run [Gemini Live](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/models/realtime/plugins/gemini/) as a comparison behind the same application contracts. Its native audio voice is a distinct option; separate ElevenLabs TTS is not a universally supported combination. Current LiveKit pages disagree on some Gemini client-managed turn configurations. Pin SDK/model versions and test the exact setup instead of combining examples from different versions.

## Application architecture

1. The app sends authenticated `POST /api/voice/sessions` with a stable request ID and privacy setting. FastAPI derives the user from the existing token, checks ownership, budget, and concurrent calls, then creates a call session and conversation. A stale duplicate request returns the same session or a clear ended state.
2. The API issues a short-lived room-scoped token and dispatches one worker. Only microphone publication is needed; no camera or phone number. Worker metadata contains a session ID; the worker loads and validates server-owned context rather than trusting a client-supplied user ID.
3. The phone connects, starts its audio session, and signals readiness. The worker greets once after readiness; user speech takes priority. A database greeting claim prevents repeated opening speech across worker retries.
4. The streaming worker calls framework-neutral memory, scripture, concern, and policy services with explicit trusted identity/privacy arguments. Both call and text modes use the same domain rules.
5. Save final user transcripts and reconciled assistant output incrementally, with stable IDs and interruption/delivery metadata. Partial captions are ephemeral. Generated text, queued audio, and played audio are different states; summaries cannot assume an unplayed recommendation reached the user.
6. End/disconnect stops media and finalizes the call. A bounded durable summary job updates context and last-session end time. If it fails, future visits use existing history; do not lose the call or wait for the job to let the user leave.
7. A later PostgreSQL-backed worker claims accepted follow-ups with leases and bounded retries. Use unique delivery keys and recheck consent/privacy before sending. External push delivery is at-least-once; suppress duplicate notifications where possible. There is no need for Redis/Celery/Temporal in the first small pilot. Add them only for measured queue/load requirements.

Use a shared history reader that normalizes text `ChatTurn` records and finalized voice messages into the same internal `ConversationMessage` shape. Keep their source IDs to avoid duplicates. This preserves the current text retry semantics without forcing interrupted speech into a completed user/reply pair. History endpoints and the drawer must support voice-only conversation entries; the present text-only message serializer will need adaptation.

All queries, caches, summaries, and jobs are user-scoped. Private mode prevents shared-memory reads/writes and follow-up creation. Audio recording is off by default at the application and transport observability layers; disclose provider processing and retention separately. Transcripts remain conversation history, not a promise that no data is retained. Deleting a conversation must invalidate its derived summaries and concern evidence; cancel dependent pending follow-ups. Account deletion must end an active room and prevent a racing worker from recreating deleted data.

A model can propose a concern or next action, but only user-stated or accepted actions become commitments. Use evidence and a trusted confirmation step for scheduled notifications. Tools are idempotent, owner-scoped, and cancellation-aware. An interruption does not necessarily roll back a tool that already executed; make transaction semantics explicit.

## Reliability and cost

No library makes this foolproof. The first release must recover predictably: interrupt speech quickly, stop cancelled output, tolerate reflective pauses, distinguish “haan/hmm” from a new request, reconnect without replaying a welcome, avoid duplicate memories, and show microphone/network failure in plain language. The [LiveKit events reference](https://docs.livekit.io/reference/agents/events/) exposes state and committed-history events, including interrupted messages. Use them as inputs, then verify playback/history accuracy on real devices; framework bookkeeping alone cannot prove what a human heard.

Proposed engineering targets for the spike, not vendor guarantees: first response audio p50 under 1.5 seconds and p95 under 3 seconds after the user's actual turn ends; speech stopping within 500 ms of an intentional interruption. Record endpointing, first-token, first-audio and client-playout timing separately. Reflective pauses can justify slower endpointing. Quality and false interruption rates matter as much as speed.

Track cost per completed conversation: room participant minutes, worker time, STT streamed minutes, model tokens, generated speech, and any optional noise processing. Idle connected calls still cost money. Cap idle/session duration and account usage, explain a limit before disconnecting, and retain access to history. Select region based on measured Indian-user network latency and provider availability. [Self-hosting LiveKit](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/self-hosting/) adds media networking, TURN, certificates, scaling and monitoring; postpone that operational burden until pilot usage justifies it. Cloud use does not remove provider/privacy review, nor do OSS licenses make model API usage free.

## Delivery sequence

**1. Prove audio before elaborate character work.** Build an isolated Android development-client spike on the current Expo/RN versions. Connect audio-only, stream Hindi/Hinglish, interrupt, reconnect, test speaker/headphones/Bluetooth, and end. Compare cascade and Gemini Live using the same prompts. Document and pin the working compatibility matrix; verify iOS separately before promising parity.

**2. Ship one scene and the existing drawer.** Implement start, connection, listening, thinking, speaking, mute, end, captions, and text fallback. Bundle a consistent small asset set. Keep controls readable against photography and expose every state/control to accessibility services. Show “AI companion” without making the screen a settings page.

**3. Add trustworthy continuity.** Persist finalized call messages, summaries and session timing. Add an ongoing concern only when useful and accepted. Implement greeting selection and one welcome per session. Test new user, same-day return, long absence, stale concern, sensitive topic, memory-off and private calls.

**4. Add accepted follow-ups after continuity works.** Start with next-visit follow-through. Add scheduled notifications with consent, neutral text, quiet hours, cancellation and outbox retries once the pilot requests them. Measure usefulness rather than increasing message frequency.

**5. Validate the product.** Recruit roughly 15 target people for a two-week directional pilot. Compare the scene/call experience with the current text experience, then compare generic openings with contextual continuity. Track completed conversations, interruptions, reported usefulness, voluntary returns, chosen actions and reported outcomes, privacy corrections, cost, and acceptance of a real paid offer. Separate visual novelty from repeated value; small-sample results are signals, not market proof.
